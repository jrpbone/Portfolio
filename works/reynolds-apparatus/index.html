Problem

A student client in the Bicol University Engineering Department needed a clear way to show how a Reynolds apparatus works and how to run it. The apparatus has many parts: tanks, a pump, valves, a dye line, a flow meter, and a clear pipe section where the flow is observed.

Students need to know what each part does, and the order of the operating procedure, before they can see laminar, transitional, and turbulent flow and calculate the Reynolds number.

What I built

I built a browser-based 3D model of the apparatus. Anyone can rotate it, tap a part to read what it does, or start a guided procedure that highlights the parts used in each step.

  • A 3D model of the frame, upper and lower tanks, pump, hoses, valves, dye injection site, flow meter, and flow visualization pipe.
  • A tap-to-learn panel that names each part and explains its purpose.
  • An 11-step operating procedure in three stages: Setup, Operation, and Cleanup. Each step highlights its parts on the model.
  • A phone layout, with the step card docked at the bottom of the screen.

How it works

Approach

The apparatus is a 3D scene in Three.js, with orbit controls to rotate and zoom. Each named part can be selected. Choosing a part or a procedure step moves the camera and highlights the matching parts, so the text and the model always point at the same thing.

System architecture

It is a static front end with no back end. Vite bundles the scene and the procedure content, and Vercel hosts the build.

Result

The simulator is live. Students can explore each part of the apparatus and follow the 11-step operating procedure on a phone or a computer.
